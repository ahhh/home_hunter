# Fire, Wildfire & Emergency Safety

Colorado's fire rules come from several agencies, and fire bans change weekly in summer. Hipcamp requires
hosts to follow local fire restrictions and to ban fires during a ban
([Host Standards](05-hipcamp-standards-checklist.md#stay-safe)).

## Who can ban fires

| Authority | Legal basis | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Board of County Commissioners** | [C.R.S. **30-15-401(1)(n.5)**](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-30/county-powers-and-functions/general/article-15/part-4/section-30-15-401/): ordinances banning open fires in unincorporated areas where wildfire danger is high | Unincorporated county, including private land |
| **County Sheriff** | C.R.S. 30-10-512, -513: sheriff is county **fire warden**; enforces county fire-ban ordinances | Unincorporated county |
| **Fire protection district** | Special district law (C.R.S. 32-1) + adopted fire code | District boundaries; open-burning permits |
| **Municipality** | Own ordinances | Inside town limits |
| **Federal / state land managers** | USFS, BLM, CPW orders | Public land next to you. Guests often cross into it. |

**Reported:** statutory citations as quoted in county fire ordinances (Park, Moffat, Boulder, Pueblo, Routt).

Most counties use **staged restrictions**:
- **Stage 1** typically bans open fires except in permanent constructed rings/grills in developed areas. Private-land rules vary.
- **Stage 2** typically bans nearly all open flame, often including charcoal. Pressurized gas stoves with shut-off valves are often still allowed.

**Read your county's actual ordinance.** Some treat private-land fire rings differently from public ones.

## Checking the current status

- The county sheriff's website or social media (most counties post there)
- The fire protection district
- The Colorado Division of Fire Prevention & Control (DFPC) fire-restriction map
- Nearby National Forest / BLM office

**Operationally:** assign someone to check fire status **daily** during fire season and before each check-in.
Build a "fire ban" message template for guests.

## Wildfire Resiliency Code (new structures)

- **[SB23-166](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb23-166)** created the Wildfire Resiliency Code Board. It adopted the **Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code (CWRC)** on **July 1, 2025**.
- Counties and municipalities with **wildland-urban interface (WUI)** areas that can adopt building/fire codes had to **adopt the CWRC as a minimum by April 1, 2026**, and be compliant by **July 1, 2026**.
- It covers **new construction and significant renovations** in the WUI: ignition-resistant construction and defensible space.
- **Reported** ([CML summary](https://www.cml.org/home/publications-news/article-detail/colorado-wildfire-resiliency-code-update), [DFPC WRCB](https://dfpc.colorado.gov/WRCB)).
- In places without a building department, DOH notes that fire-related reviews may be done by fire districts, DFPC or approved third parties.

If you plan cabins, yurt platforms, bathhouses or decks in the WUI, expect CWRC requirements.

## Wildfire risk and defensible space

- Check the parcel on the **Colorado Forest Atlas / Wildfire Risk Public Viewer** (Colorado State Forest Service).
- CSFS's Home Ignition Zone guidance: 0-5 ft noncombustible, 5-30 ft lean-clean-green, 30-100 ft thinned fuels. Apply the same idea around **campsites and fire rings**.
- Keep fire rings well away from trees and overhanging branches. Clear to mineral soil around the ring. Keep water and a shovel at every ring.

## Site equipment checklist

| ☐ | Item | Type |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Fire rings (if allowed): steel, sunk, cleared ~10 ft, no overhead branches | Best practice |
| ☐ | Bucket of water + shovel at each ring | Best practice (often in ordinances) |
| ☐ | ABC fire extinguisher at each structure and at a central post | Best practice / fire code for structures |
| ☐ | Smoke alarm + **carbon monoxide alarm** in every sleeping structure with a fuel-burning appliance | CO law ([structures](09-structures-tiny-homes-rvs.md)) |
| ☐ | Propane: cylinders secured, stored outside, shut-offs labeled | Best practice / fire code |
| ☐ | Generator rules (hours, distance from tents, spark arrestor) | Best practice |
| ☐ | "No fireworks." Fireworks are restricted in Colorado and often banned during fire restrictions. | Local law |

## Emergency access and evacuation

- Fire apparatus needs a drivable route. Typical fire-code targets are ~20 ft wide, with turnarounds on long dead ends and grades within limits. Ask your fire district. See [Access](13-access-parking.md).
- Have **two ways out** where possible. Post a map at the entrance showing evacuation routes.
- Sign up for the county's **emergency alert system** (most use CodeRED, Everbridge or similar) and tell guests how to sign up.
- **Cell coverage:** test at each site. If there's none, provide a landline, satellite messenger or posted directions to the nearest signal.

See the [Emergency & Incident Plan](16-emergency-incident-plan.md) for the printable plan.

## Communicating with guests

Put in the listing and the pre-arrival message:
- Current fire-restriction stage, and that it can change mid-stay
- Whether fires are allowed at all, and where
- Propane-only alternatives
- Weather: afternoon lightning, flash floods in drainages, wind
- The evacuation route and the alert sign-up link
