# Road Access, Parking & Emergency Access

Hipcamp expects **off-street parking for all guests** and attention to how traffic affects neighbors. Colorado
counties and fire districts have their own access requirements, and those often become conditions of approval.

## Legal access

| ☐ | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Does the parcel touch a **public** road (county, state, or a municipal road that's maintained)? | Only public roads carry a right of access. A "County Road" number doesn't prove the county maintains it. |
| ☐ | If not, is there a **recorded easement** to a public road? | Physical access without a recorded right can be cut off |
| ☐ | Does the easement's **scope** cover commercial/guest traffic? | Many are residential or agricultural only ([property rights](11-property-rights-hoas-easements.md)) |
| ☐ | Is there a **road maintenance agreement**? | Cost sharing; limits on commercial use |
| ☐ | Seasonal closure? Is the county road maintained in winter? | Many mountain roads aren't plowed. Guests get stuck. |
| ☐ | Gates: who controls the code? Is fire/EMS access guaranteed? | Emergency responders must get in |

## Driveway and access permits

- **County driveway/access permit:** most counties require one to connect to a county road. **Park County** requires a permitted, finaled driveway and a visible address before camping on vacant land. Clear Creek requires legal access from a **county-maintained public road** for commercial camping. Both **Verified** ([Park](counties/park.md), [Clear Creek](counties/clear-creek.md)).
- **State highway:** access to a state highway is governed by the **State Highway Access Code** ([2 CCR 601-1](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/DisplayRule.do?action=ruleinfo&ruleId=2162)) and CDOT access permits. A **change in use** of an existing access (e.g. residential to commercial campground traffic) can require a new access permit. Ask the CDOT region access manager.
- **Addressing:** get a rural address assigned (county or 911 authority) and post it where it's visible from the road. Emergency responders depend on it.

## Parking

| ☐ | Item |
|---|---|
| ☐ | Off-street spot(s) at or near each site; no parking on county roads or shared easements |
| ☐ | Surface fit for the season (mud, snow), with room for trailers to turn around |
| ☐ | Vehicle limit per site, stated in the listing |
| ☐ | Parking well clear of dry grass (hot exhaust systems can start fires) |
| ☐ | Night lighting kept low (see dark-sky notes in [Environment](14-environmental-constraints.md)) |

## Emergency-vehicle access

Ask the **fire protection district** which fire code applies. Common targets (confirm locally):

- All-weather driving surface about **20 ft wide** for apparatus, with **13'6"** vertical clearance
- **Turnarounds** on dead-end drives beyond a set length
- **Grade** limits, and bridge/culvert load ratings
- Gate access for responders (Knox box or code on file)
- Posted site numbers, plus a site map at the entrance

## Traffic and neighbors

- Estimate trips: sites × check-ins, plus supply runs. Share the estimate with neighbors on shared roads.
- Post a speed limit and a "residents live here" sign on the private road (Hipcamp asks for this).
- Send clear arrival directions so guests don't wander onto neighbors' property.
- Put a quiet-arrival window in the rules (e.g. no arrivals after 10pm).

## Winter and weather

- Say in the listing whether 4WD/AWD or chains are needed, and when the road is closed.
- Flash floods can wash out fords and low crossings. Include this in [Fire & Emergency](08-fire-and-emergency-safety.md) messaging.
