# Colorado Water & Well Compliance

In Colorado, **having a well doesn't mean you can use its water for paying guests.** A well permit lists
specific uses. Using the water for anything else is a violation, and for campgrounds the gap between
"household" and "commercial" use is usually the problem.

Main source: **DWR Guideline 2023-1, *Uses of Water From Exempt and Small Capacity Wells*** (Division of Water
Resources), read in full 2026-09-29. **Verified.** Supersedes DWR Policy 2011-3.

## Look up the well permit first

1. Search the DWR well permit database (via [DWR well permitting](https://dwr.colorado.gov/services/well-permitting)) by location or permit #.
2. Note the **permit number**, **statute** (e.g. 37-92-602(3)(b)(II)(A)), **allowed uses**, and **acreage basis**.
3. No permit on record? The well may be unregistered or pre-1972. Registering it is a separate DWR process.

## What the common permit types allow

| Permit type | Typical allowed uses | Guests? |
|---|---|---|
| **Household use only** (most exempt wells on parcels **under 35 acres** since May 8, 1972; not cluster lots) | Ordinary household purposes **inside one single-family dwelling**. No outdoor use. | **No** for campers (see below) |
| **Domestic / exempt, 35+ acres** | Up to 3 single-family dwellings, 1 acre of lawn/garden, domestic animals, livestock | Only as part of normal dwelling use |
| **Exempt commercial** ([37-92-602(1)(c)](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-37/water-rights-and-irrigation/water-right-determination-and-administration/article-92/part-6/section-37-92-602/)) | Drinking and sanitary facilities **inside an individual commercial business**. Evaluated under DWR Policy 1985-1. | Possibly. Ask DWR. |
| **Small capacity** (inside Designated Basins, 37-90-105) | Up to 3 dwellings, livestock, **one commercial business**, ≤1 acre irrigation. Ground water management district rules may restrict. | Possibly |
| **Non-exempt / large capacity** | Whatever the permit/decree says. Often requires an **augmentation plan** in over-appropriated basins. | Designed for it, at a cost |

## What DWR says about guests, businesses and rentals

Quoted or paraphrased closely from Guideline 2023-1 (**Verified**):

- On property served by a well **without a commercial use**, business activity is fine only if "no additional water will be diverted or consumed as a result of the business." Specifically, **employees (other than residents) and customers cannot use water from the well**, and the business can't use the water to conduct business (§2.1).
- **Renting the house** (short- or long-term) to people using it like a single family counts as household use (§3).
- An **auxiliary living space with a separate entry and kitchen** is generally a **second** single-family dwelling. Short-term rental units used intermittently or seasonally "may qualify for small capacity or exempt permits (similar to a small hotel)." DWR may allow a **commercial exempt** permit to serve the main house plus the rental unit (§3).
- **Not** ordinary household use, and not allowed on exempt/small-capacity household or domestic permits: **event venues**, **bed and breakfasts, hotels, motels, hostels**, boarding houses, and properties where **customers** use water because of an onsite business (§3).
- Filling hot tubs/pools and outdoor washing are not household use (§3).

**What this means for Hipcamp:** paying campers are *customers*. If they fill jugs from a household-use
well, use a spigot, or shower from it, that is almost certainly outside the permit. Options:

1. **No water provided.** Guests bring their own. Say so clearly in the listing.
2. **Hauled potable water** from a licensed source, stored in food-grade tanks.
3. **Change or add a permit** (exempt commercial, small capacity, or non-exempt). Talk to DWR. May need an augmentation plan.
4. **Municipal/district water** where available. Confirm the provider allows commercial use.

## When your water becomes a "public water system"

Under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act, adopted in Colorado's Primary Drinking Water Regulations ([5 CCR 1002-11](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/DisplayRule.do?action=ruleinfo&ruleId=3179)),
a system that serves **at least 25 people per day for at least 60 days a year** (or has 15+ service connections)
is a **public water system**. A campground that provides drinking water can hit this as a *transient
non-community* system. That brings monitoring, a certified operator and CDPHE oversight. **Reported.**
Confirm thresholds with CDPHE's Water Quality Control Division.

## Other Colorado water points

- **Rain barrels:** Colorado allows up to two barrels (110 gallons total) at single-family/small residential properties, for **outdoor** watering only. They aren't a guest water supply. **Reported.**
- **Cisterns** filled by hauled water are generally fine for household use but need potable-grade handling if guests drink the water.
- **Surface water** (creek, pond): no right to divert it without a water right. Don't offer it as drinking water.
- **Water rights and augmentation:** growing beyond household use in over-appropriated basins (most of the state) may need a court-approved augmentation plan. That's expensive and slow. Talk to a water attorney before paying for land on the assumption that you can add water later.

## Questions for DWR

1. What uses does permit # ____ allow?
2. Can paying campers use water from this well under the current permit? If not, which permit type would allow it?
3. Would a small cabin rented intermittently qualify under a commercial exempt permit (Policy 1985-1)?
4. Is the parcel in a Designated Basin or ground water management district?
