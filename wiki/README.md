# Colorado Camping & Hosting Wiki: Start Here

This wiki goes with Home Hunter. It helps answer one question about a Colorado property:

> **Can we legally host paying campers (Hipcamp) here, and what would it take?**

It covers what counts as a "home" under Colorado zoning, how each county handles camping, what Hipcamp
requires of hosts, and the Colorado laws that tend to decide the answer: septic, wells, fire, taxes and access.

> **This is research, not legal advice.** Every statement shows a source and a date. Rules change, and
> a county page checked in 2026 isn't authoritative in 2029. Before you buy, build or list, confirm with the
> county planning department, the local public health agency and, for anything significant, a Colorado
> land-use attorney.

## Who makes the rules

Several layers of rules apply to every parcel. A higher layer can be stricter than a lower one, but it
can't make something legal that a lower layer bans.

```
Colorado state law            statutes, CDPHE / DWR / DOR / DFPC rules
  └─ County                   zoning & land-use code for UNINCORPORATED land
       └─ Municipality        replaces county zoning if the parcel is INSIDE a city/town
            └─ Special districts   fire protection, water & sanitation, metro districts
                 └─ Private restrictions   HOA covenants, deed restrictions, easements,
                                           conservation easements, leases, lender terms
                      └─ Hipcamp           platform standards (a contract, not law)
```

**Researching the county isn't enough if the parcel is inside a city or town.** Inside a municipality,
the town's zoning code applies instead of the county's. Check the assessor record first.

## Three categories that aren't the same

Keep these three separate throughout your research. Treating them as one leads research in the wrong direction.

| Category | Plain meaning | Typical rules |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Residence / home** | Where someone lives: a dwelling unit | Zoning (dwellings per lot), building code, OWTS sized for bedrooms, household-use well |
| **(b) Overnight accommodation** | A place a *guest* sleeps for a short stay (<30 days) | Camping-duration limits, STR/vacation-rental permits, lodging taxes |
| **(c) Campground / commercial lodging business** | An operation offering sites or units to the public for money | Campground/RV-park zoning (often special use), CDPHE campground sanitation, commercial well, business licensing |

A Hipcamp listing is always (b). Depending on how many sites you have, whether you charge, and how the
county defines it, it may also be (c). It isn't (a), even if the guest sleeps in a cabin. See
[What Counts as a Home](01-what-counts-as-a-home.md).

## Evidence standard

Every regulatory statement in this wiki carries one of these statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Read in the primary source (statute, rule, adopted code, official agency page). Section cited. |
| **Reported** | From an official page or search summary, or a reputable secondary source, but the adopted text wasn't read in full. |
| **Needs confirmation** | A lead only. Ask the agency. |
| **Not researched** | Nothing looked at yet. |

On the [Property Worksheet](04-property-worksheet.md), each item's result uses: **Verified / Needs confirmation /
Permit required / Potential conflict / Not researched**. The worksheet never gives a plain yes/no.

## In the app

Clicking a county on the Home Hunter map shows a **Hosting campers (Hipcamp)** section. It gives the county's
signal, a one-line summary, whether it's been checked against the county's own documents, links to the primary
sources, and a link back to the county's page here. That data (`public/data/states/CO/hosting.json`) is generated
from the same source file as the county pages. The panel also has a **Tax-credit zones** section with the
county's enterprise zone, opportunity zone and Rural Jump-Start status, from the same data as
[Tax-Credit Zones](18-incentive-zones.md).

## Maintaining the wiki

- Record every source in [Sources & Change Log](17-sources-and-changelog.md) with the date checked and the next review date.
- County pages are **generated**. Edit `pipelines/hosting/counties.json`, then run `python3 pipelines/hosting/build.py`. See [County Matrix: Editing](counties/README.md#editing). CI fails if the pages are out of date.
- Review county pages every 6 months. Review Hipcamp standards and the tax pages every 12 months, or sooner if something changes.
- Every county claim cites a numbered source labeled **primary** (the county's or state's own document) or **secondary**.
  A claim is **verified** only when all of its sources are primary and were actually read.
