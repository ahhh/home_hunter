# Colorado Taxes, Business Licensing & Lodging Taxes

**Camping revenue isn't tax-exempt in Colorado.** Campsites and host-provided shelters are taxable
"rooms and accommodations."

Main source: Colorado Department of Revenue, [Sales & Use Tax Topics: Rooms & Accommodations](https://tax.colorado.gov/sales-use-tax-topics-rooms-accommodations),
read 2026-09-29. **Verified.**

## What's taxable

- Colorado sales tax applies to **any regular sleeping room or unit** in a hotel, inn, lodging house, guest house, motel, **mobile home**, dude/guest ranch.
- It also applies to **space in any auto camp, or trailer court or park**, under any permit, license or agreement.
- An **auto camp** is "a temporary, overnight lodging accommodation that specifically caters to persons traveling by motor vehicle," offering one or more of these amenities:
  - vehicle electricity supply,
  - vehicle water supply,
  - a vehicle sewage and waste water dump station, or
  - **a temporary or permanent overnight shelter (such as a tent, yurt, teepee, or other shelter) provided by the owner or operator.**
- An accommodation whose **only** amenity is EV charging isn't taxable as an auto camp.
- Older DOR guidance also listed "temporary overnight parking facilities" as an amenity. The current page (checked 2026-09-29) doesn't.
- Tax applies to the **entire charge**, including cleaning fees and non-optional add-ons.
- **Cancellation charges / forfeited deposits:** taxable if more than 50% of the daily rate. Not taxable at 50% or less.
- **Exempt:** stays of **30+ consecutive days** by a permanent resident under a written agreement; certain government and charitable renters.

> **Reading this for Hipcamp:** any site with electric, water or a dump station, and any host-provided tent,
> yurt, teepee or cabin, is clearly taxable. A **bare site where guests bring everything** (no hookups, no
> shelter) is the gray area under the current wording. Hipcamp collects the state-administered taxes on
> bookings anyway, so the question matters mainly for **off-platform bookings**. Ask DOR before treating any
> site as exempt.

## Who collects what

| Tax | Administered by | Collected on Hipcamp bookings by |
|---|---|---|
| State sales tax (2.9%) | DOR | **Hipcamp** (marketplace facilitator) |
| State-administered county, city and special-district sales taxes | DOR | **Hipcamp** |
| **County lodging tax** (C.R.S. 30-11-107.5) | DOR | **Hipcamp** |
| **Local marketing district tax** (C.R.S. 29-25-112) | DOR | **Hipcamp** |
| Regional transportation authority visitor benefit tax | DOR | Hipcamp, if state-administered. Confirm. |
| **Home-rule city** sales and lodging taxes | The city itself | **Host, unless Hipcamp lists that city.** Check. |

- DOR (**Verified**): "The marketplace facilitator is required to collect and remit the applicable state and state-administered sales taxes, as well as any applicable county lodging tax and local marketing district tax." DOR "does not administer any lodging taxes imposed by any municipality."
- Hipcamp (**Reported**, from its support article): for bookings after **September 9, 2024**, Hipcamp collects and remits Colorado's state and county occupancy taxes and some city taxes. Hosts are responsible for any other local taxes. Hipcamp keeps a list of the Colorado counties and cities it collects for: [Which Colorado Counties and Cities is Hipcamp collecting taxes for?](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/30155369660052-Which-Colorado-Counties-and-Cities-is-Hipcamp-collecting-taxes-for).

## Licenses

| License | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Colorado sales tax license** (DOR) | DOR: "Anyone who offers rooms or accommodations for rent is required to obtain a sales tax license." | Ask DOR whether you still need one if **every** booking goes through a marketplace that collects. Needed anyway for direct/off-platform bookings. |
| **Home-rule city** sales/lodging license | Parcel inside a home-rule city | Separate registration with the city |
| **County STR / vacation rental permit** | Structures rented short-term (e.g. Archuleta, Custer, Mesa) | Many counties now license STRs |
| County/municipal **business license** | Some jurisdictions | Hipcamp mentions business-license fees |
| **Electronic filing** | County lodging tax filers: mandatory e-file for 2026 if prior-year sales were $75,000+, for 2027 if $7,000+, and for everyone from Jan 2028 | Mainly for direct bookings, since Hipcamp files its own |

## Income and property tax

- **Federal/state income tax:** all rental income is reportable. Hipcamp issues 1099s at the federal thresholds it lists ($20,000 and 200 bookings). Keep records: gross bookings, platform fees, taxes collected by Hipcamp, and expenses.
- **Property tax:** commercial lodging use can change the **assessment classification** of part of the property (Colorado assesses lodging differently from residential). Also check whether hosting affects any **agricultural classification**. Ask the county assessor before you start.
