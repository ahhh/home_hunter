# Subdividing Land, Zoning & Short-Term Rentals

This page covers three Colorado **state** law questions a land buyer runs into:

1. Can I split this parcel, and what does the state require when I do?
2. What can a county do with zoning, and what has the state taken away from counties?
3. Can a cabin on this land be rented on Airbnb or VRBO?

County details live on the [county pages](counties/README.md). This page explains the state rules that every
county works inside. It builds on [Jurisdiction & Zoning](02-jurisdiction-and-zoning.md),
[Water & Wells](07-water-and-wells.md), [Structures](09-structures-tiny-homes-rvs.md) and
[Taxes](10-taxes-and-licensing.md), and doesn't repeat them.

> **This is research, not legal advice.** Statute text below was read on 2026-10-03 in
> [colorado.public.law](https://colorado.public.law/statutes), which reproduces the official C.R.S.
> ([2024 C.R.S. Title 30 PDF](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-30.pdf)).
> Later amendments may not be shown. Check any section you rely on against the current official text.

## Bottom line for a buyer

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| **Can I split my land?** | **Into pieces of 35+ acres each: generally yes, without county subdivision review.** State law says such a division isn't a "subdivision" ([C.R.S. 30-28-101(10)(b)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-101)). **Verified.** Into anything smaller: only through county **subdivision** review, a county **subdivision exemption**, or a county **cluster / rural land use** process. **Verified.** |
| **What usually stops a small split?** | **Water.** In most of the state, a new lot under 35 acres can't get its own exempt well without a court-approved augmentation plan ([DWR Beginner's Guide](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1axZVUaG9QGdBLebzM6F0YOut2MrL4pI5/view)). **Verified.** Then septic (soils), legal road access, and the county's zoning minimum lot size. |
| **Is a 35-acre split really approval-free?** | **Not in practice.** Every piece, *including the remainder*, must be 35+ acres. The county still decides access, addressing, driveway, building and septic permits, and it can refuse permits on a parcel that wasn't legally created. Many counties also zone agricultural land at a **35-acre minimum**, so smaller pieces aren't allowed by zoning anyway. **Reported.** |
| **What if the land was split illegally before I buy?** | It may not be a **legal lot**. Expect no building permit until it's fixed, and the seller may have committed a misdemeanor ([30-28-110(4)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-110)). **Verified** (statute). |
| **Can a cabin be an Airbnb?** | **Only if it's a lawful dwelling (or approved lodging unit) and the county allows STRs there.** There's **no statewide STR law**. Counties may license STRs ([30-15-401(1)(s)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-15-401)). Many rural counties don't license them at all; others require a permit, cap them, or ban them in some zones. Add HOA covenants, septic sized for the bedrooms, a well permit that allows the use, CO alarms, and lodging taxes. **Verified** (state law); county rules vary. |

## Part 1: Subdividing land

### What counts as a "subdivision"

[C.R.S. 30-28-101(10)(a)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-101) (**Verified**): a "subdivision" is any parcel
"which is divided into two or more parcels, separate interests, or interests in common," **or** any parcel "to be used for
condominiums, apartments, or any other multiple-dwelling units" (unless an earlier compliant filing already allowed
that density). "Interests" means surface interests only.

Anything that meets this definition needs county subdivision approval **unless** one of these applies:

| Exclusion | Statute | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Every parcel is 35+ acres** and none is "intended for use by multiple owners" | 30-28-101(10)(b) | The "35-acre rule." **Every** resulting parcel counts, including what you keep. |
| Each parcel, divided by its number of interests, is 35+ acres per interest | (10)(c)(I) | E.g. 70 acres with two undivided interests. |
| Created by eminent domain, operation of law, or court order (county gets notice and 20 days to object to evasion) | (10)(c)(II) | Partition suits included, with county notice. |
| Created by a lien, mortgage, deed of trust or other security instrument | (10)(c)(III) | Borrowing against part of the land isn't a split. |
| Interests in an investment trust or investment entity | (10)(c)(IV) | |
| Cemetery lots | (10)(c)(V) | |
| Severed oil, gas, mineral or water interests | (10)(c)(VI) | |
| Buying land as joint tenants or tenants in common (counts as **one** interest) | (10)(c)(VII) | Friends can co-buy without "subdividing." |
| **Combining** contiguous parcels | (10)(c)(VIII) | If the result is under 35 acres, only one interest is allowed. |
| A purchase contract contingent on getting subdivision approval | (10)(c)(IX) | Lets you go under contract before approval. |
| A **cluster development** under Part 4 | (10)(c)(X) | See below. |
| **Anything else the county exempts** "by rules and regulations or resolution" because it's "not within the purposes" of the law | **30-28-101(10)(d)** | This is where county "subdivision exemptions" come from. |

All of (10)(c) applies only "unless the method of disposition is adopted for the purpose of evading" the law. **Verified.**

**Boundary-line adjustments, lot-line eliminations, splits for a family member, and one-time "minor" splits are not
in the statute.** Counties create them under (10)(d), each with its own name, size limits and conditions. **Verified**
(the statute has no such exclusion); what each county offers is on the [county pages](counties/README.md).

**Selling cabins as separate units** (a condominium or "cabin condo" on one parcel) is itself a subdivision under
(10)(a), because the land is "to be used for condominiums ... or other multiple-dwelling units." **Verified.** It needs
county subdivision review before units are sold, and the common-interest-community rules in C.R.S. 38-33.3 apply
on top. Renting cabins short-term is a different question (Part 3).

### The 1972 law and "legal lots"

- **History.** Colorado's subdivision law is known as **Senate Bill 35** (1972). Larimer County: "May 5, 1972 is the date that Senate Bill 35, C.R.S. 30-28-101, became state law," and any parcel under 35 acres created after that without county approval "is an illegal parcel" ([Larimer, Property Status Information, 2023](https://larimer.gov/sites/default/files/legal_lot_101.pdf)). **Reported** (county document, not the session law).
- **Statute.** Every county had to adopt subdivision regulations "not later than September 1, 1972" ([30-28-133(1)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-133)). **Verified.**
- **Different dates in different places.** Counties commonly use **May 5, 1972**. DWR uses **May 8, 1972** for well permits and **June 1, 1972** for subdivision creation dates ([37-92-602](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_37-92-602); [DWR Beginner's Guide](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1axZVUaG9QGdBLebzM6F0YOut2MrL4pI5/view)). **Verified.** Know which date each agency uses.

A **legal lot** (county term, not a statutory one) is typically a parcel that was (Larimer's definition, **Reported**):

1. created by a deed or recorded plat **before May 5, 1972**;
2. created by county approval (plat or exemption) under the rules in effect at the time;
3. a parcel of **35 acres or more which, when created, didn't leave a parcel under 35 acres behind**; or
4. under a signed purchase contract dated before May 5, 1972.

Item 3 is the common trap: **splitting 50 acres into 35 + 15 makes the 15 illegal**, and arguably taints the deal.
Gunnison County likewise requires owners to show a lot is "legal" before it issues a building permit
([Gunnison, Legal Lot](https://www.gunnisoncounty.org/418/Legal-Lot)). **Reported.**

**Buyer step:** ask the county planning office for a **legal lot / parcel status determination** before closing.
Pull the deed chain back to 1972 (a title company can do this).

### What county subdivision regulations must cover

[C.R.S. 30-28-133](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-133) (**Verified**). Each county must require the subdivider to submit:

| Item | Subsection |
|---|---|
| Survey; surface **and mineral** ownership, incl. mineral lessees | (3)(a) |
| Site reports: streams, topography, vegetation, geology, radiation hazard, **soil suitability** | (3)(b) |
| A plat: dwelling units, gallons/day of water and sewage (or "sewage disposal means and suitability" where there's no central plant), road and utility costs, stormwater plans | (3)(c) |
| **"Adequate evidence that a water supply that is sufficient in terms of quality, quantity, and dependability will be available"** (water rights, yields, a provider's commitment, potability) | **(3)(d)** |
| Electric and gas utility easements and access | (3)(e) |

The regulations must also cover, at minimum (4):

- **School and park sites**: reservation, **dedication**, or a **fee in lieu** up to the land's fair market value (4)(a).
- Storm drainage standards, sized to the 100-year storm (4)(b).
- Sewer standards, including **percolation testing** and on-lot septic site design (4)(c).
- Water system standards (4)(d).

No preliminary plan or final plat may be approved without evidence of an adequate water supply, a sewage plan
that complies with state and local law, and identification of soil and topographic hazards (6). Every lot must
also have access to the state highway system under the state access code
([30-28-133.1](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-133.1)). **Verified.**

### Plats, referrals and selling before approval

- **Plats.** Subdivision plats go to the county commissioners. It's "not lawful to record" one without their approval ([30-28-110(3)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-110)). Unpaid property taxes block approval (4)(a). **Verified.**
- **Referrals.** The county must send the preliminary plan to ([30-28-136](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-136), **Verified**): school districts; any county or town within **2 miles**; utilities, districts and **ditch companies**; the **State Forest Service** when applicable; the local **conservation district** (soils, floods); the **health agency** (sewage and water quality; the plan **can't be approved** without its favorable sewage recommendation); the **State Engineer** (water injury and adequacy); and the **Colorado Geological Survey**. Agencies have 21 days to respond.
- **State Engineer's opinion.** If the county approves despite a negative State Engineer opinion, the subdivider must give buyers a copy or synopsis before sale ([30-28-136(1)(h)(I)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-136)). **Verified.** Ask for it.
- **Selling before approval.** A subdivider who sells or transfers subdivided land **before the final plat is approved and recorded** commits a **misdemeanor**: a fine of **$500-$1,000 per parcel**. Prosecution must start within 18 months of recording ([30-28-110(4)(a)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-110)). The county can get an injunction (4)(b). A violation is "prima facie evidence of a fraudulent land transaction" and **the buyer may void the sale** (4)(d). **Counties may enforce by withholding building permits** (4)(a). This penalty section applies **only to parcels under 35 acres** (4)(e). **Verified.**
- **Improvement guarantees.** No final plat is recorded until the subdivider posts a **subdivision improvements agreement** with collateral, or another agreement, for roads and other required public improvements. Buyers can sue to enforce plat restrictions and plat notes ([30-28-137](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-137)). **Verified.** (The *sale-before-approval* penalty is in 30-28-110(4), not 30-28-137.)

### Cluster development / rural land use process

[C.R.S. 30-28-401 to -404](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-403) (**Verified**). An option **only where the county has adopted a "rural land use process"**:

- Creates lots **under 35 acres**, **single-family residential only** (403(1)).
- **At least two-thirds** of the tract is reserved as **open space**. Open space is protected from development for **at least 40 years** (403(1)-(2)).
- Density cap: **one unit per 17.5 acres** (403(1)). Counties may give density bonuses up to **two units per 35-acre increment** (401(1)(c)).
- Water: **one well permit per lot** (404(1)). Outside designated basins and areas with unappropriated water, a **water-court augmentation plan** is required if use would exceed **1 acre-foot per year per 35 acres** (404(2)). The county must notify the State Engineer within 10 days of approval (404(3)).
- Cluster developments are excluded from the "subdivision" definition (101(10)(c)(X)), so standard subdivision review doesn't apply. The rural land use process does.

Example: 70 acres could become 4 homes on small lots, with ~47 acres left as open space. Not every county offers this.

### Inside a town: municipal subdivision

Inside a city or town, the **town's** subdivision rules apply ([C.R.S. 31-23-214](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_31-23-214)). **Verified.**
The statute lets towns regulate streets, open space, utilities, fire access and **minimum lot area and width**. It
has no 35-acre exclusion like the county statute, so towns set their own definitions. **Needs confirmation** for a
specific town. Home-rule cities follow their charters.

**Annexation.** If the land is annexed into a town, the town's zoning and subdivision code take over. A buyer
splitting land near a town should know that (a) the county must refer plans within **2 miles** of a town to that town
([30-28-110(5)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-110), [30-28-136(1)(b)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-136)), **Verified**; and (b) towns often ask for an
annexation agreement before they'll extend water or sewer. **Reported.**

### Do 35+ acre splits really need nothing from the county?

The statute exempts them from **subdivision** review. It doesn't exempt them from anything else. In practice
(**Reported**, from county codes and the pattern on the county pages):

- **Zoning still applies.** If the zone's minimum lot size is 35 acres, 35+ acre pieces are fine. If it's 40 or 80 acres, they aren't, even though they aren't a "subdivision."
- **Building permits are issued only on legal lots.** Counties check the deed history. The **remainder** must also be 35+ acres.
- **Access.** Each new parcel needs **legal and physical access**: a recorded easement or public road frontage, and a county or CDOT **driveway/access permit**. See [Access](13-access-parking.md). Landlocked 35-acre parcels are common and hard to fix.
- **Surveys and addresses.** Many counties require a recorded survey (land survey plat), new parcel numbers from the assessor, and a 911 address before permits.
- **Water.** Each 35+ acre parcel can usually get one **domestic exempt well** (see below). A smaller split usually can't.
- **Sold as "35-acre ranchettes" with covenants.** Large-lot developments often carry private road maintenance agreements and covenants (see [Property Rights](11-property-rights-hoas-easements.md)).

## Part 2: Water and splitting land

The water rules are what make 35 acres the magic number for rural buyers.

### Exempt wells

[C.R.S. 37-92-602](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_37-92-602) (**Verified**) exempts small wells (≤15 gpm) from the priority system:

- **(1)(b) domestic:** ordinary household use, fire protection, poultry, domestic animals and livestock, up to **1 acre** of lawn and garden, **no more than 3 single-family dwellings**.
- **(1)(c) commercial:** drinking and sanitary facilities in an individual commercial business.

For applications since **May 8, 1972**, DWR presumes no injury to other water rights (so it will issue the permit) in
these cases ((3)(b)(II)(A)):

- the only well on a residential site, used **only for ordinary household purposes inside one single-family dwelling**, no irrigation; **or**
- the only well on a tract of **35 acres or more**; **or**
- the only well on a **cluster development** lot, within the 1 acre-foot per 35 acres limit.

For lots in a **subdivision approved on or after June 1, 1972** whose water plan the State Engineer didn't approve,
DWR must consider the **cumulative** effect of all wells in the subdivision ((3)(b)(III)). In practice that means no
exempt well without an augmentation plan in most basins.

### What DWR says this means

From DWR's **Beginner's Guide to Well Permits** (updated October 2025), read 2026-10-03 (**Verified**):

| Parcel | Typical well permit |
|---|---|
| **35+ acres**, not in a post-June 1, 1972 subdivision or augmentation plan, the only exempt well on that 35+ acres | **Domestic**: up to 3 homes, 1 acre lawn/garden, domestic animals and livestock |
| **Under 35 acres**, created **before June 1, 1972**, or **created by a county exemption** from subdivision | **Household use only**: inside one single-family home, no outside use, no livestock. Usually only one exempt well. |
| **Lot in a subdivision created after June 1, 1972** | Governed by the subdivision's **augmentation plan**. Uses beyond it need water court. |
| Designated Basins (8 on the eastern plains) and the Denver Basin | Different statutes. Domestic uses may be available on smaller parcels. Check. |
| Areas **not over-appropriated** (a few on the western slope) | Domestic uses may be available on smaller parcels |

DWR's guide, under "Wells and Dividing Land": "In most areas of the state, you would be unable to obtain a well
permit to serve a new lot or lots created through a subdivision process without first obtaining an augmentation
plan." That applies to lots under 35 acres created by subdivision **or** by a county exemption under
30-28-101(10)(d). **Verified.**

**Splitting a parcel that already has a well** ([SB20-155](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb20-155), per DWR's guide): an existing
35-acre-presumption well keeps its permit after a split **only for one of the new parcels**, if it stays that parcel's
only exempt well. The other new parcels can't share it or get their own exempt well. They need a water tap or a
non-exempt permit. **Verified** (DWR guide).

**DWR won't pre-approve.** DWR "cannot confirm that a particular well permit will be issued without completing an
evaluation." It needs an application and fee. It reviews subdivision water plans only on a county's referral
([DWR, Land Divisions & Water Supply](https://dwr.colorado.gov/services/water-administration/land-divisions-and-water-supply)). **Verified.** A seller's "it can get a well" isn't evidence.

For what a well permit allows once you have one (guests, rentals, businesses), see [Water & Wells](07-water-and-wells.md).

### Adequate water supply for larger developments

[C.R.S. 29-20-301 to -305](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_29-20-303) (**Verified**): a county or town "shall not approve an application for a
development permit unless it determines in its sole discretion ... that the proposed water supply will be adequate"
(303(1)). The applicant files an engineer's water supply report (304). But for this part, "development permit" is
limited to projects with new water use **above 50 single-family equivalents**, "or fewer as determined by the local
government" ([29-20-103(1)(b)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_29-20-103)). So a small rural split is usually reviewed under the subdivision
water rule (30-28-133(3)(d)) rather than this one, unless the county lowered the threshold.

## Part 3: The zoning framework

### What counties can do

- **Zoning plan.** The county planning commission may prepare a zoning plan covering building height, bulk and size, lot coverage, **lot size**, population density, and the use of land and buildings for "trade, industry, residence, recreation" ([30-28-111(1)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-111)). **Verified.**
- **Zoning resolution.** After a public hearing, the commissioners may regulate those things by resolution "in any portions of such county that lie outside of cities and towns," divide the county into **districts**, and **require building permits** before any building is built or altered ([30-28-113(1)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-113)). Rules must be uniform within a district (113(2)). **Verified.**
- **Purposes.** Zoning must promote health, safety and welfare, including "fostering the state's agricultural and other industries" and "protecting the tax base" ([30-28-115(1)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-115)). **Verified.**
- **Zoning is optional.** The statute says the commission "may" zone. A few rural counties have little or no zoning. **Verified** (statute wording); which counties is on the [county pages](counties/README.md).
- **1041 powers.** Counties can also designate areas and activities of state interest and require a separate permit inside them. See [Jurisdiction & Zoning § 7](02-jurisdiction-and-zoning.md#7-areas-and-activities-of-state-interest-1041).

### What counties can't do

| Limit | Statute / bill | Applies to rural county land? |
|---|---|---|
| **Can't exclude factory-built homes**: DOH-certified, HUD manufactured, or homes equal to the county code on a performance basis | [30-28-115(3)(b)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-115) **Verified** | Yes. Counties can still regulate them like other homes in the zone. |
| **Can't exclude or out-regulate factory-built structures**; must follow DOH's standards for them | [SB25-002](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25-002) (signed May 8, 2025) **Reported** | Yes, as summarized on the bill page |
| **Licensed group homes** (8 persons, developmental disability, mental health) are a residential use; homes for the aged must be allowed | [30-28-115(2)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-28-115) **Verified** | Yes |
| **No occupancy limits based on family relationship** | [HB24-1007](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1007) **Reported** | Yes (all local governments, per the bill summary) |
| **Must allow one ADU** next to a single-family home, by administrative approval, from June 30, 2025; no owner-occupancy rule; no new parking requirement | [HB24-1152](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1152), C.R.S. 29-35-101 et seq. **Verified** (signed act) | **Generally no.** It covers only "subject jurisdictions": towns of 1,000+ people inside a metropolitan planning organization (MPO), and **the part of a county inside a census designated place of 40,000+ people inside an MPO** (29-35-102(21)). Rural unincorporated land isn't covered. |
| Transit-oriented housing density | [HB24-1313](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1313) **Reported** | No. Aimed at transit areas in metro cities. |
| Minimum parking near transit | [HB24-1304](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1304) **Reported** | No. Metro transit areas. |

**HB24-1152 and STRs.** Even where the ADU law applies, a subject jurisdiction may still regulate or ban STRs of the
ADU or the main house (29-35-103(3)(g)), and may require the owner to live on the parcel to get an STR license
(103(2)(b)(II)). **Verified.** So the ADU law isn't an STR loophole.

**2026 session.** The headline housing bills signed in 2026 (HB26-1001, the "HOME Act," housing on land owned by
nonprofits, schools and districts; SB26-001, workforce housing tools for local governments) don't change county
subdivision or zoning rules for private rural land
([Governor's release, March 25, 2026](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/governor-polis-signs-laws-breaking-down-barriers-new-homes-coloradans-can-afford)). **Reported.** No 2025 or 2026 bill changing the 35-acre rule was found. **Needs confirmation** (search only).

## Part 4: Short-term rentals (Airbnb, VRBO)

### Is there a state STR law?

**No.** Legislative Council Staff (January 2026): "There are no statewide regulations regarding STRs." State law
defines an STR as a rental of a lodging unit for **less than 30 days** and leaves regulation to counties and towns
([Short-term Rental Regulation, LCS Issue Brief, Jan 2026](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/r25-881-shortterm-rentals-accessible.pdf)). **Verified.** There is no state preemption: a county may license, limit or ban STRs.

### County power to license STRs

[C.R.S. 30-15-401(1)(s)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_30-15-401) (**Verified**), added by [HB20-1093](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb20-1093) and revised by [HB23-1287](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb23-1287):

- Counties may "license and regulate an owner or owner's agent who rents or advertises the owner's lodging unit for a short-term rental," and set fees, terms, and revocation (s)(I).
- Counties may make platforms show the **local license number** on listings, and make them **remove a listing within 7 days** after the county says the license was revoked or suspended, the owner has no license, or the county **prohibits STRs** for that unit (s)(III).
- Licensing **doesn't change** whether the unit is "residential" for property tax (s)(II).
- "Lodging unit" is any property or part of one available for lodging, except a hotel unit. "Short-term rental" is under 30 days (s.5).

Counties also use **zoning** (where STRs are allowed, as what use) alongside licensing. Home-rule towns regulated STRs
before 2020 under their own powers (LCS brief). **Verified.**

Note: [HB22-1117](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb22-1117) is often cited for STRs. It didn't create licensing power. It lets voters approve spending **county lodging tax** on workforce housing, childcare and visitor services. **Verified** (bill page).

### What can lawfully be rented as an STR

The state doesn't create a separate "STR building." A rental cabin has to be lawful on its own terms:

1. **A permitted dwelling or an approved lodging unit.** A cabin with a kitchen and bath is a dwelling unit. It must fit the zone's density (often one home, maybe one ADU) and have building/DOH permits. A sleeping-only cabin usually isn't a dwelling and needs a lodging or campground approval. See [Structures](09-structures-tiny-homes-rvs.md#cabins). **Reported** (county-code pattern).
2. **Septic sized for the bedrooms.** Under CDPHE Regulation 43, residential design flow is at least **75 gallons per person per day**, figured on bedrooms, with a two-bedroom minimum ([5 CCR 1002-43.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/colorado/5-CCR-1002-43.6)). **Reported.** Reg 43 was revised in 2026; check the current version and the local health agency's rules. Adding a rental cabin usually means a new or expanded OWTS permit. See [Toilets & Septic](06-toilets-septic-waste.md).
3. **A well permit that covers it.** DWR treats renting a house to people who use it like a family as household use; a second unit with its own kitchen is a **second dwelling** and may need a different permit. See [Water & Wells](07-water-and-wells.md). **Verified** (DWR Guideline 2023-1).
4. **A county STR license**, where the county requires one, and zoning that allows the use.
5. **Wildfire Resiliency Code** for new construction in the WUI. See [Fire](08-fire-and-emergency-safety.md#wildfire-resiliency-code-new-structures).

### Taxes on stays under 30 days

From DOR, [Rooms & Accommodations](https://tax.colorado.gov/sales-use-tax-topics-rooms-accommodations), read 2026-10-03 (**Verified**):

- State sales tax (and state-administered city, county and district sales taxes) applies to the **whole charge**, including cleaning fees.
- Exempt only if the guest is a **permanent resident** with a written agreement for **30+ consecutive days**.
- "Anyone who offers rooms or accommodations for rent is required to obtain a sales tax license."
- On Airbnb/VRBO bookings, "the marketplace facilitator is required to collect and remit the applicable state and state-administered sales taxes, as well as any applicable **county lodging tax** and **local marketing district tax**."
- DOR "does not administer any lodging taxes imposed by any municipality." Home-rule city taxes are separate.

Full detail, filing, and the e-filing thresholds are in [Taxes](10-taxes-and-licensing.md).

### Property tax

| Point | Status |
|---|---|
| A building "designed for use predominantly as a place of residency" is a **residential improvement**. Tiny homes and manufactured homes count ([39-1-102(14.3)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_39-1-102)). | **Verified** |
| "Hotels and motels" (lodging property, assessed at a much higher rate) **exclude a residential unit** unless it's a **"hotel unit"**: generally **more than four** residential units in one project owned by one person or related group and used for public lodging ([39-1-102(5.5)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_39-1-102)). | **Verified** |
| Bed and breakfasts have their own rules ([39-1-102(2.5), (3.1), (5.6)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_39-1-102)). | **Verified** |
| Bills to tax STRs as lodging property **failed**: [SB24-033](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-033) (defeated April 2024) and [HB24-1299](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1299) (postponed indefinitely April 22, 2024). Several websites wrongly say HB24-1299 took effect in 2026. | **Verified** (bill page for HB24-1299); **Reported** for SB24-033 |
| 2026 residential assessment rate for local government levies: **6.80%** after a 10% reduction of the first $700,000 of value ([DPT](https://dpt.colorado.gov/residential-local-government-assessment-rate)). | **Verified** |
| An STR cabin on **agricultural** land: the ag classification covers the land in ag use, not a residence or rental use. Ask the assessor how a rental cabin changes classification. | **Needs confirmation** |

So for 2026: one cabin rented short-term is still **residential** for property tax under state law. The county
can't change that by licensing it. **Verified** (30-15-401(1)(s)(II), 39-1-102).

### HOA covenants

- Use, occupancy and rental restrictions belong in the **recorded declaration** ([C.R.S. 38-33.3-205(1)(l)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_38-33.3-205)). **Verified.**
- An amendment that "change[s] the uses to which any unit is restricted" needs **at least 67%** of the association's votes ([38-33.3-217(4.5)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_38-33.3-217)). **Verified.**
- Colorado's Court of Appeals has held that an HOA board can't ban STRs by rule. The covenants themselves must be amended. **Reported** (secondary summary of case law).
- **Buyer step:** read the declaration and every amendment for "rental," "lease," "commercial" and "business" clauses. Old rural covenants often ban any business use. See [Property Rights](11-property-rights-hoas-easements.md).

### Safety and insurance

- **Carbon monoxide alarms.** Any single-family dwelling or unit "used for rental purposes" with **fuel-fired appliances or an attached garage** must have CO alarms when tenants change (on or after July 1, 2009) or when permitted work is done ([38-45-104(1)-(2)](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_38-45-104)). The standard is an alarm within 15 feet of each sleeping room, or as the local building code says ([38-45-103](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_38-45-103)). The owner must replace missing or broken alarms before each new occupancy (104(3)(a)). **Verified.** The statute talks about "tenants"; it doesn't name STRs. Treat it as applying, since every booking is a change in occupancy. **Needs confirmation** for a formal ruling.
- **Smoke alarms** come from the building and fire codes the county or fire district adopted, and county STR licenses usually list them. There's no general state smoke-alarm statute for STRs. **Reported.**
- **Insurance.** A standard homeowner policy often excludes business or rental use. Get an STR or commercial policy. Platform host protection isn't a substitute. See [Insurance](12-insurance-and-liability.md). **Reported.**

## How this plays out by county

Most of the variation between counties falls into a few patterns. See the [County Matrix](counties/README.md) for each county.

**Splitting land**

| Pattern | What it looks like | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **35-acre ag zoning** | The agricultural zone's minimum lot is 35 acres (sometimes 40 or 80). Below that, rezoning **and** subdivision. | "What is the minimum lot size in this zone?" |
| **Exemption paths** | Minor split, family split, lot-line adjustment, "rural land division," each under 30-28-101(10)(d) with limits (how many lots, how often, minimum size). | "What exemption processes do you offer, and has this parcel already used one?" |
| **Cluster / rural land use process** | Some counties offer Part 4 clustering for smaller lots with open space. Many don't. | "Do you have a rural land use process?" |
| **Full subdivision** | Sketch plan → preliminary plan → final plat, with state referrals. Many months; engineering, water and fees. | "What does a 2-lot minor subdivision cost and how long does it take?" |
| **Legal-lot checks** | Permits only on lots shown to be legally created. | "Can you issue a parcel status determination for APN ____?" |

**Short-term rentals**

| Pattern | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **No STR program** | No license. STRs are treated as an ordinary residential use, or the code is silent. Silent isn't the same as allowed; get it in writing. |
| **Permit / license program** | Annual license, inspection, occupancy limits, parking, local contact, license number on listings. Often cheaper for owner-occupied units. |
| **Caps or zone limits** | Limited licenses, or STRs allowed only in some zones (e.g. Boulder County caps whole-house vacation rentals at 277 licenses in its mountain zones, per the LCS brief). |
| **Structure limits** | Bans on renting RVs, tents or accessory structures as STRs (e.g. San Miguel, Mesa; see [Structures](09-structures-tiny-homes-rvs.md)). |

## Questions to ask before buying

1. Is this parcel a **legal lot**? On what basis (pre-1972 deed, plat, exemption, 35+ acres with no small remainder)?
2. What's the **zoning** and its **minimum lot size**? Could I split it, and by which process?
3. What **well permit** is on the parcel (number, statute, allowed uses)? Would a split parcel get a well? (Ask DWR, and expect "only after you apply.")
4. Does every resulting parcel have **recorded legal access** and a driveway permit path?
5. Does the county **license STRs**? In this zone? For a detached cabin? With what occupancy and septic rules?
6. Do **covenants** limit rentals, business use, or further splits?
7. Is the parcel inside or near a **town** (2-mile referral, annexation)?
