# Property Rights, HOAs, Easements & Neighbor Issues

Zoning can allow something that a private agreement still forbids. The private layer is enforced by
neighbors, HOAs and land trusts in civil court, and a title search is the only reliable way to find all of it.

## What to pull before buying

| ☐ | Document | Where |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | Title commitment with **all Schedule B exceptions** | Title company (ask for copies of every recorded document listed) |
| ☐ | Recorded plat and plat notes | County Clerk & Recorder |
| ☐ | Declaration / CC&Rs, and HOA rules and bylaws | Clerk & Recorder; HOA |
| ☐ | Easements (access, utility, drainage, ditch) | Title / Clerk & Recorder |
| ☐ | Conservation easement deed and baseline report | Clerk & Recorder; the land trust |
| ☐ | Road maintenance agreement | Title / neighbors / HOA |
| ☐ | Leases (grazing, hunting, mineral, solar) | Seller disclosure |
| ☐ | Survey / ILC | Surveyor |

## HOAs and covenants

- Colorado HOAs are governed mainly by the **Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act** (CCIOA, [C.R.S. 38-33.3](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-38/real-property/interests-in-land/article-33-3/)) and their recorded declarations.
- Look for clauses on: **commercial use or business**, **short-term rentals**, **camping / tents / RVs** ("no temporary structures," "no RV occupancy"), **number of vehicles**, **noise**, **signs**, **fires**, and **minimum rental terms**.
- Rural "covenant-only" subdivisions (no active HOA) still have enforceable covenants. Any lot owner may be able to sue.
- Hipcamp notes that HOA permission may be required. **Ouray County** requires proof the HOA was notified before issuing a long-term camping permit. **Reported.**

## Conservation easements and land trusts

- Colorado has a lot of conservation-easement land, much of it donated for state tax credits.
- Easement deeds often **limit commercial recreation, new structures, building envelopes, roads and parking**. Many need **land trust approval** for any new use.
- Hipcamp notes that land-trust permission may be necessary. Get it **in writing** before listing.

## Access easements and shared roads

- Confirm **legal** access (recorded easement or public road), not just physical access. See [Access](13-access-parking.md).
- Many easements are written for **residential** or **agricultural** use. Paying guests may **exceed the scope** of the easement. Neighbors can object, and courts read easement scope strictly.
- Shared road maintenance agreements often split costs by use. Commercial traffic may change your share or be prohibited.

## Deed restrictions and severed rights

- Deed restrictions can ban commercial use, mobile homes/RVs, or further subdivision.
- **Mineral estate:** in Colorado the mineral estate is often **severed**, and surface-use agreements with oil and gas operators can affect where you can put sites. Check the title.
- **Water rights** don't automatically pass with land unless they're deeded. See [Water](07-water-and-wells.md).

## Lenders and leases

- Residential mortgages often limit **commercial use**, and owner-occupancy riders may be breached by rentals. Read the deed of trust.
- **Grazing or hunting leases** may give others rights to be on the land during your guests' stays, which is a safety problem during hunting season.

## Neighbors

Hipcamp requires you to notify neighbors, especially where roads are shared, traffic increases or sites are
visible. In practice, **neighbor complaints are what trigger most county enforcement**. Before you list:

- Meet the neighbors on shared roads. Explain the site count, the quiet hours, and how to reach you.
- Place sites away from property lines and out of neighbors' sight lines.
- Give neighbors a direct phone number for problems.
- Remember that special/conditional use hearings include **public notice to neighbors**. Their support or opposition matters.
