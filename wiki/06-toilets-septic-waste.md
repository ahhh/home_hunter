# Colorado Toilets, Septic & Human-Waste Requirements

Sanitation is the requirement most likely to sink a raw-land campsite. Three sets of rules overlap: the
state's septic rules (enforced locally), the county's camping/campground conditions, and Hipcamp's toilet policy.

## Who regulates it

| Layer | Rule | Role |
|---|---|---|
| State | **CDPHE Regulation 43** ([5 CCR 1002-43](https://cdphe.colorado.gov/OWTS)), On-site Wastewater Treatment Systems | Minimum statewide OWTS standards. Revised in 2026. [Boulder County's summary of the 2026 changes](https://bouldercounty.gov/environment/water/septicsmart/2026-owts-regulation-changes/). |
| Local | **Local public health agency** regulations (must be at least as strict as Reg 43) | **Permits OWTS up to 2,000 gallons/day**: septic, privies, composting/incinerating toilets. [Find your agency](https://cdphe.colorado.gov/public-information/find-your-local-public-health-agency). |
| State | CDPHE Water Quality Control Division | Systems over 2,000 gpd (a large campground can get there) |
| State | **CDPHE campground rule** ([6 CCR 1010-9](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/DisplayRule.do?action=ruleinfo&ruleId=2429)) | Sanitation standards for campgrounds. Primitive and semi-primitive campgrounds are exempt from most sections. Ask your local agency whether and how they enforce it. |
| County | Zoning/camping code | Often **makes sanitation a condition of camping** (see below) |
| Platform | Hipcamp toilet policy | Tier A-E; the 20-acre rule ([checklist](05-hipcamp-standards-checklist.md#toilet-policy-in-detail)) |

**Verified:** Reg 43 structure and local permitting (from CDPHE and county sources); 6 CCR 1010-9 definitions and exemptions.
**Needs confirmation:** how each local agency applies 6 CCR 1010-9 to small private campgrounds.

## County sanitation conditions (examples)

| County | Condition | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [Park](counties/park.md) | Sewage goes to an approved off-site facility, a permitted OWTS, or an **incinerating, composting or portable toilet properly disposed** | Verified |
| [Lake](counties/lake.md) | Sewage to a facility or onsite system **permitted/approved by Lake County Environmental Health** | Verified |
| [Chaffee](counties/chaffee.md) | Commercial private-lands campsites may use **off-site disposal (wag bags, RV tanks, portable toilets)**. Onsite facilities must be permitted under Reg 43. | Verified |
| [Ouray](counties/ouray.md) | Long-term camping needs approved sanitation (**no compost toilets**). Allowed days depend on the type: personal porta-potty 30, commercial porta-potty 180, OWTS up to 365. | Verified |
| [Routt](counties/routt.md) | Commercial campgrounds with 1-3 spaces need a dump station, toilets and potable water. 4+ spaces also need hookups and showers. | Verified |
| [Saguache](counties/saguache.md) | You need a **county-approved septic system** to live or stay on the property, and a temp RV permit requires an installed OWTS | Reported |
| [Alamosa](counties/alamosa.md) | RV Temporary Use Permit requires an **installed OWTS** and a legal water source | Verified |
| Rio Grande | No RV may be inhabited for **any period** without adequate sewage disposal | Reported |
| Ouray | Long-term camping sanitation facility must be **screened** from neighbors and roads | Reported |
| Lake (case) | Campground owners asked for a **variance** to use vault/composting toilets. The board delayed a decision. | Reported |

The lesson: **"bring your own bucket" camping may satisfy Hipcamp on 20+ acres and still break county rules.**

## Options compared

| Option | Hipcamp tier | Colorado permitting | Good for | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing house bathroom (guest access) | D/E | Existing OWTS permit | 1-2 sites near the house | OWTS **sized for bedrooms**. Added guest load may exceed the design. Guests in your house. |
| New/expanded **OWTS** + bathhouse | D/E | Local OWTS permit, soils test, designed by an engineer | Long-term operation, cabins | Cost ($15k-$40k+ in the mountains), percolation, high groundwater, setbacks from wells/streams |
| **Vault privy** (sealed tank, pumped) | D | Local OWTS/privy permit | Remote tent sites | Some counties restrict or require variances. Needs pumping-truck access. |
| **Composting toilet** (NSF/ANSI 41) | D/E | Local approval. Often still needs a graywater plan. | Low-water sites | Approval varies. Maintenance burden. |
| **Incinerating toilet** | D/E | Local approval; needs power/propane | Cabins | Energy cost |
| **Portable toilet** (contracted service) | C | Usually no OWTS permit; service contract | Seasonal / startup | Weekly service cost; truck access; wind tie-downs |
| Guest's **self-contained RV** | B | County RV rules; dump-station plan | RV-only sites | Only works if **every** guest is self-contained |
| Guest's **camp toilet** (bags/bucket) | A | Often **not** "adequate sewage disposal" under county rules | 20+ ac tent-only on Hipcamp | **Not allowed by Hipcamp** where you provide any sleeping accommodation |
| Pit privy (unlined) | — | Heavily restricted / often not permitted | — | Groundwater contamination risk. Assume no. |

## RV waste, graywater and dumping

- Discharging sewage (black or gray tank) onto the ground or into waterways is illegal. Point guests to the nearest **public dump station** and put it in your guest rules.
- **Graywater** (dishwater, showers) is wastewater under Colorado rules unless your local government has adopted a graywater program under **CDPHE [Regulation 86](https://www.sos.state.co.us/CCR/DisplayRule.do?action=ruleinfo&ruleId=3242)**. Most rural counties haven't. Plan a dishwater strainer plus a sanctioned disposal point, or route it to the OWTS.
- A **dump station** you build for guests is part of the "auto camp" taxable amenities list ([taxes](10-taxes-and-licensing.md)) and needs its own health approval.

## Mapping county rules to Hipcamp

| Situation | Hipcamp says | Colorado likely says | Do this |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tent-only, <20 ac | Permitted toilet required | Permitted/approved facility required | Portable toilet (Tier C) minimum; vault/OWTS long-term |
| Tent-only, 20+ ac | Guests may bring their own | Many counties still require approved sanitation | Ask the local health agency. Don't rely on BYO. |
| Self-contained RV dry camping | No host toilet needed | RV rules + dump plan | Require self-contained units in the listing; list dump stations |
| Any host-provided shelter (bell tent, yurt, cabin) | Tier B-E required | Structure + sanitation approval | OWTS, vault or portable toilet within ~55 yd |

## Questions for the local public health agency

1. For paid camping of [N] tent/RV sites, what sanitation do you require?
2. Do you permit **vault privies** and/or **composting toilets** here? What do you require (NSF 41, graywater)?
3. Does my existing OWTS (permit #) have capacity for guests? Does adding guests count as a change of use?
4. Do you apply **6 CCR 1010-9** (campgrounds) to a site of this size? Which sections?
5. Does a portable toilet with a service contract meet your requirements, and for how long?
