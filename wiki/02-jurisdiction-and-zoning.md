# Colorado Jurisdiction & Zoning Guide

Answer these questions in order for every parcel. Each answer changes which rules apply to the next one.

## 1. Is the parcel inside a city or town?

Colorado has 64 counties and about 270 incorporated municipalities. **Inside a municipality, the city or town
does the zoning**, under its own code (C.R.S. 31-23-301 et seq., or its home-rule charter). **Outside,
in unincorporated land, the county does** (county zoning under [C.R.S. 30-28-111](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-30/county-planning-and-building-codes/article-28/part-1/section-30-28-111/) et seq.).

How to check:
- Find the parcel on the **county assessor's** site. The tax-district / "taxing entities" list names the town if there is one.
- Mailing addresses and ZIP codes are **not** reliable. A "Salida" address can be in unincorporated Chaffee County.
- **Denver and Broomfield** are consolidated city-counties, with one code for the whole county.

If the parcel is inside a town, the county page in this wiki is only background. Pull the town's code
(most are on Municode or eCode360) and ask the town planner.

## 2. What kind of county is it?

| Type | Counties | What differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Statutory county** | Most of the 64 | Powers come from state statute. Zoning under C.R.S. 30-28. |
| **Home-rule county** | Weld, Pitkin | Own charter. Zoning still follows the state framework, but check the charter/code. |
| **City and county** | Denver, Broomfield | Municipal code covers everything. |

## 3. What zone district is the parcel in?

- Use the county GIS/zoning map, or ask Planning for a **written zoning verification** (many counties sell one for a small fee; it's worth it before you buy).
- Note the district's name *and* its use table: which uses are "by right," "accessory," "temporary," "special/conditional," or "not allowed."
- Check for **overlay districts**: floodplain, wildfire, scenic byway, wildlife habitat, airport, 1041 areas.
- Check for a **PUD / Planned Development**. PUD parcels follow their own approved plan, which often bans commercial uses.

## 4. How does the code treat camping?

Look for each of these terms in the definitions and the use table:

| Term to search | Why |
|---|---|
| camping / camping unit / temporary occupancy | Duration limits for non-commercial camping |
| campground / camping area / RV park / travel trailer park | The commercial pathway |
| small camping facility / private land camping / agritourism | Newer, lighter-weight pathways (Garfield, Chaffee) |
| recreational vehicle / temporary dwelling | Whether an RV can be occupied at all |
| guest ranch / lodge / resort / bed and breakfast / vacation rental / short-term rental | Lodging-use pathways that may cover cabins |
| temporary use permit | Seasonal or event allowances |
| home occupation | Usually **doesn't** cover guests staying overnight. Check. |

### Use categories

| Category | Meaning for Hipcamp |
|---|---|
| **Use by right** | Allowed if the standards are met (setbacks, sanitation, time limits). May still need an administrative permit. |
| **Accessory use** | Allowed only alongside a principal use (usually the home). Often limits "camping" to the owner's guests. |
| **Temporary use** | Time-limited (days per year). Rarely supports an ongoing listing. |
| **Special / conditional use** | Needs a public hearing (Planning Commission, then the Board of County Commissioners), conditions, neighbor notice. Months, and often thousands of dollars. |
| **Not listed / prohibited** | Many codes, including Adams, say that an unlisted use is **not allowed** unless the director rules it similar to a listed one. |

## 5. Does paying, or adding sites, turn it into a campground?

This is the most important question. It's the one people most often get wrong. Common triggers seen in Colorado codes:

- **Money changes hands.** Clear Creek defines "Private Camping" as camping where *no money or thing of value* is exchanged. Paid camping is "Commercial Camping" and is only allowed in commercial-recreation zones ([Clear Creek](counties/clear-creek.md)). Park County's rules allow only the owner and "invited, non-paying guests" in residential zones ([Park](counties/park.md)). Routt limits private camping to "non-paying personal guests" ([Routt](counties/routt.md)). Ouray's long-term camping requires that "no fee" be charged ([Ouray](counties/ouray.md)). Gilpin says renting campsites isn't a use by right anywhere ([Gilpin](counties/gilpin.md)). All **Verified** in the county's own documents.
- **Number of units.** Lake County reportedly treats **2+ camping units** as a campground. Routt defines commercial camping as "one or more" spaces open to the public. CDPHE's campground categories start at **2+ campsites**.
- **Being open to the public.** CDPHE covers private campgrounds "made available, either with or without a fee to the public."
- **Explicit rental bans.** San Miguel County bars renting RVs, camping spaces and accessory structures as short-term rentals. Mesa County's vacation-rental rules bar tents and RVs as guest accommodations. Rio Grande County's 2024 amendments aimed at "dry camping" for commercial gain.

Each claim is cited on the linked county page.

**What to do:** ask Planning in writing, *"How do you classify paid overnight camping, booked through an online
platform, for [N] sites on parcel [APN] in the [district] zone?"* Keep the reply.

## 7. Areas and activities of state interest (1041)

Under **[C.R.S. 24-65.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-24/planning-state/article-65-1/part-4/section-24-65-1-401/) ("1041 powers")**, counties can designate areas or activities of state interest (wildlife
habitat, natural hazard areas, historic areas and others) and require an extra 1041 permit inside them.
Kiowa County, among others, has 1041 regulations. Ask whether the parcel sits in any 1041 area. **Reported.**

## 8. Legal lot

Only a **legal lot** can get land-use permits. In Colorado that generally means a parcel created before
**September 1, 1972** (when the state subdivision law took effect), created through county subdivision review,
or created by an exemption. Parcels of **35 acres or more** fall outside the statutory definition of
"subdivision" ([C.R.S. 30-28-101(10)](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/2022/title-30/article-28/part-1/section-30-28-101/)). Archuleta County says outright that it issues land use permits only for
a legal lot. **Reported.** Illegally split parcels are common in rural Colorado. Check before you buy.

## Hipcamp's view

Hipcamp tells hosts that regulations are commonly found in zoning codes, building codes and county/local
ordinances, and that "there may be state, county, or local laws that regulate or even prohibit hosting
Hipcampers on your property." The host is responsible for finding out. Hipcamp's listing policy also says
homes in suburban and residential areas "aren't appropriate," and that residential neighborhoods are
generally unsuitable unless the property has substantial acreage. See the
[Hipcamp Checklist](05-hipcamp-standards-checklist.md).
